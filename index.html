02 · Flagship course

Financial literacy, in six stops

A short course you can finish in one sitting or one class period. Move through it module by module — every trap on this site is built out of these ideas.

01

What is credit

Credit, debt and interest

You have almost certainly borrowed money already — a “pay in 3 parts” checkout, ₹200 off a friend, food ordered now and settled later. This module gives you the three words that describe all of it. Everything else on this site is built on them.

Credit
Someone lets you use money now that you do not have yet. Not a gift, not income — a promise with a date attached.
Debt
The total you owe because you took that credit: what you borrowed, plus everything charged on top. Debt is not evil. Debt you never worked out is.
Interest
Rent on someone else's money. Charged as a percentage over time — which is why the same loan costs more the longer you hold it.
02

How interest actually works

Simple vs. compound

Two loans can advertise the identical rate and cost you thousands apart. The difference is how that rate is applied — to what you first borrowed, or to what you still owe. Every trap further down this site is built on that difference.

Simple interest
Charged only on what you first borrowed. ₹1,000 at 10% for two years costs ₹200. Predictable, straight-line, and increasingly rare.
Compound interest
Charged on everything you owe, including interest already added. Over ten years at 24%, ₹10,000 costs ₹34,000 simple — and ₹85,944 compound.
Flat vs reducing balance
“12% flat” charges you on the full amount for the whole term, even as you repay it. “12% reducing” charges only on what is left. Same headline. Different price.
03

The three-party lending structure

Who is really lending

The single most important fact about the app on your phone: it usually is not the lender. Indian regulation splits digital lending into three roles, and knowing which one you are talking to decides whether a complaint lands or gets bounced between two companies forever.

The real lender
A bank or NBFC licensed by the RBI — a Regulated Entity. It owns the loan, carries the risk, and is finally answerable for everything done in its name.
The app's operator
The company that builds the app, markets it, signs you up and often makes the recovery calls. A service provider, not your lender — and it cannot hide behind that.
The app itself
Just the interface. Every one must be reported to the RBI, so an app you cannot trace back to a licensed lender is a red flag, not a bargain.
04

Spotting manipulation tactics

The brain traps

Most of these products are legal and many are run by licensed lenders. The danger is not that they are criminal — it is that they are engineered, and you are the one who has not read the design. Three patterns do most of the work.

No friction
No forms, no wait, no one asking what it is for. Friction is what makes a person pause. Remove it and you remove the pause.
Framing
“₹299 a month” instead of “₹3,588 a year plus fees.” Both describe the same product. The first number you see becomes the one you judge everything else against.
Present bias
The reward is today; the cost is a rumour called “later.” People reliably choose the smaller thing now over the bigger thing later. Instant credit is built on that gap.
05

Red flags and the PDC model

Green, yellow, red

Two skills catch almost everything: turn any fee into a yearly percentage, and check any repayment against what you can actually afford. This module gives you both — and the checklist that tells you when to stop reading and walk away.

The calculation that never fails
Borrow ₹2,000 for 15 days, fee ₹100. That is 5% — for 15 days. There are about 24 such periods in a year. 5% × 24 ≈ 122% a year.
Red flags
No Key Fact Statement. No lender name anywhere. Money into a wallet instead of your bank. Requests for contacts or photos. Automatic limit increases. Three means walk.
The Personal Debt Ceiling
Stable monthly income × a conservative safety factor, minus what you already owe each month. Every new instalment gets checked against it before you agree, not after.
06

If you're already in trouble

Getting out, safely

Borrowing to repay is the trap closing. If that has already happened, this is the module that matters: what to stop doing today, what your rights actually are, and exactly who to contact. None of it costs anything.

Stop the stacking
App B to pay App A, then App C to pay App B — each one pricier, because each is lending to someone visibly in trouble. The moment you borrow to repay, stop.
What they may never do
Call before 8 a.m. or after 7 p.m. Contact your family, friends or classmates about your debt. Threaten, shame or send fake legal notices. All of it is already illegal.
Where to go
Screenshot everything. Tell one adult today. Complain to the lender's Grievance Officer. Escalate at cms.rbi.org.in. For threats: cybercrime.gov.in, or call 1930.
03 · Original research

How these platforms actually work

De-Trap's own analysis of BNPL and instant-loan products in India: who really lends the money, what the fees actually are, and what happens if you miss a payment. Open a card for the full breakdown.

Amazon Pay Later

Active
BNPL
RBI-linked lending partners

Amazon acts only as a Loan Service Provider (LSP). The actual credit comes from two RBI-regulated lenders, and Amazon's 2025 acquisition of one of them narrows the gap between platform and lender.

Full breakdown

Real lender

Axio (formerly CapFloat), co-lending with Karur Vysya Bank, and IDFC FIRST Bank. Users are assigned to one lender via Amazon's internal allocation after PAN + Aadhaar KYC. In 2025 Amazon acquired Axio, its first ownership stake in an NBFC.

Fee structure

  • Credit limit up to ₹60,000 for shopping and bills
  • Zero interest if the monthly bill is paid in full and on time
  • Optional EMI conversion (3–12 months) carries nominal interest, set by the lending partner

Rollover / missed payment

  • Missed or late payment triggers a late fee plus interest, charged by Axio/KVB or IDFC FIRST, not Amazon
  • Missed payments are reported to credit bureaus (CIBIL/Experian)
  • Recovery and collections are handled by the lending partner, not Amazon support

Regulatory status

Governed by RBI's Digital Lending Guidelines through its bank/NBFC partners, not Amazon directly. Amazon's acquisition of Axio in 2025 reduces the separation between the shopping platform and the entity legally responsible for the loan.

Source: De-Trap platform workflow analysis, 2026

Simpl

Halted
BNPL
Core checkout halted by RBI order, 25 Sept 2025

One of India's earliest BNPL apps, built on a frictionless one-tap checkout and a running “khata” ledger. Unlike its peers, Simpl never operated through a licensed bank or NBFC — and that gap is exactly what shut it down.

Full breakdown

Real lender

None. Simpl settled directly with merchants itself instead of routing credit through an RBI-regulated bank or NBFC — the structural feature that separated it from peers like Amazon Pay Later or LazyPay.

Fee structure

  • Instant spending limit from Simpl's own in-house underwriting, no card, password or OTP at checkout
  • Purchases across 26,000+ merchants pooled into one running balance
  • 15-day repayment window at zero interest if paid on time

Rollover / missed payment

  • Late fees of roughly ₹50 to ₹100 if the 15-day window is missed
  • Accounts suspended, and merchants notified, until the outstanding balance clears

Regulatory status

RBI found Simpl was operating a payment system without the Certificate of Authorisation required under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. On 25 September 2025, RBI ordered it to halt those operations immediately — the clearest evidence yet of RBI closing the “quasi-payment system” loophole that let BNPL apps lend without a licensed partner.

Source: RBI order via The Paypers and AngelOne (Sept 2025); GrabOn and Fintech Singapore India BNPL reviews (2026)

LazyPay

Active
BNPL / pay-later credit line
Runs on a licensed NBFC (PayU Finance)

The useful contrast to Simpl: LazyPay looks like a one-tap pay-later button, but the credit behind it is issued by a registered NBFC, not the app maker. That single fact is what kept it standing when the unlicensed models were shut down.

Full breakdown

Real lender

PayU Finance India Pvt Ltd — PayU's RBI-registered NBFC arm. “LazyPay” is the consumer brand; the loan itself is underwritten and owned by the NBFC (with partner lenders in some flows). There is always a licensed entity on record.

Fee structure

  • A pay-later limit with an interest-free window if the bill is cleared on time
  • EMI conversions and personal loans carry interest — commonly quoted from the high-teens up to ~30%+ a year, set by the lender on your profile
  • Processing / convenience fees on longer-tenure loans

Rollover / missed payment

  • Late fees plus penal charges once the window is missed
  • Missed payments reported to credit bureaus, denting your wider score
  • Collections handled by the NBFC and its partners, not the app's support desk

Regulatory status

Governed by RBI's Digital Lending Directions through PayU Finance. RBI's June 2022 circular — which barred loading credit lines onto prepaid instruments — pushed pay-later players to route credit through NBFC lending rather than a wallet; LazyPay's licensed-NBFC structure is exactly that model.

Source: PayU / LazyPay public disclosures; RBI PPI circular (June 2022); RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2022. De-Trap analysis, 2026.

KreditBee

Watch
Instant personal loan app
Pure instant-loan category · under scrutiny

Not BNPL at a checkout — a straight cash loan into your account in minutes. This is the category where loan-stacking and aggressive recovery cluster, which is exactly why the “turn any fee into a yearly rate” skill matters most here.

Full breakdown

Real lender

Operated by KrazyBee Services Pvt Ltd, lending mainly through its group NBFC and co-lending partners. Small-ticket loans from about ₹1,000 up to a few lakh, with tenures from a couple of months to a few years.

Fee structure

  • A processing fee deducted up front — so the amount that lands is less than the amount you owe
  • Interest commonly quoted up to ~29.95% a year, varying by product and profile
  • Short tenures turn even a “small” fee into a very high yearly rate

Rollover / missed payment

  • Late and penal charges; extensions raise the total fast
  • The classic stacking trap — a second app to clear the first — lives in this category
  • Repayments reported to credit bureaus

Data practice & regulatory status

Instant-loan apps historically asked for broad phone permissions; RBI's 2022 Digital Lending Directions now restrict data access to what a loan actually needs. In 2023 the Enforcement Directorate searched premises linked to several app lenders, KreditBee among them, in a wider FEMA probe; the company said it cooperated and had moved to Indian ownership. Governed by RBI's Digital Lending Directions through its NBFC.

Source: KreditBee product disclosures; Enforcement Directorate search reporting (2023); RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2022. De-Trap analysis, 2026.

Slice

Transformed
Prepaid card + credit line → small finance bank
Reshaped by RBI's June 2022 PPI circular

The clearest case of a regulator forcing a model to change rather than die. Slice built a hugely popular prepaid card with a credit line for young users — then a single RBI circular pulled the mechanism out from under it, and the company had to rebuild.

Full breakdown

Original model

A prepaid payment instrument — a card and wallet — loaded from a credit line via an NBFC partner, marketed as a friendly “pay in 3” card aimed squarely at students and first-jobbers.

What changed

  • In June 2022 RBI barred non-bank prepaid instruments from being loaded with credit lines
  • That instantly broke the core Slice mechanic, and the product was reworked toward EMIs and UPI
  • Slice then merged with North East Small Finance Bank (RBI-approved, effective 2023) — moving inside a regulated bank rather than shutting down

Why it's on this page

It shows regulation working as a scalpel, not a hammer: the same June 2022 circular that reshaped Slice is why licensed players now route credit through NBFCs and banks. Read any “card + credit line” pitch with this history in mind.

Source: RBI PPI Master Directions and June 2022 clarification; RBI approval of the Slice–North East Small Finance Bank merger (2023). De-Trap analysis, 2026.

04 · De-Trap's internal tool

The PDC model

Predict, detect, correct. De-Trap's own guidebook framework for talking about risk — a conversation starter for workshops, not a validated academic instrument.

Predict

Before money moves

Spot the conditions that make borrowing likely before any money changes hands: no savings buffer, pressure to keep up, unclear costs.

Detect

Once it has started

Notice the signs once borrowing has begun: missed dates, hidden apps, borrowing to repay.

Correct

The next concrete step

The specific next step for each zone, from a checklist to simply telling one adult.

Clear
No live debt, costs understood, a buffer in place.
Exposed
Borrowing without the maths, or a payment already late.
Trapped
Borrowing to repay, hiding it, being contacted about money owed.
This is a De-Trap teaching tool built for workshops, not a clinical or academic diagnostic. Treat the zone as a conversation starter, not a verdict.
